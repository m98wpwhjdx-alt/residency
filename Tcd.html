Transcranial Doppler (TCD)

Overview

Transcranial Doppler (TCD) is a non-invasive ultrasound technique used to measure cerebral blood flow velocity in the major basal cerebral arteries, most commonly the middle cerebral artery. It provides real-time information about cerebral hemodynamics and autoregulation.

Key Points

  • Purpose: Measures blood flow velocity in cerebral arteries to assess cerebral blood flow and cerebral autoregulation.
  • How it works: Uses a low-frequency ultrasound probe, typically around 2 MHz, placed over thin cranial acoustic windows such as the temporal, orbital, and suboccipital windows.
  • Commonly assessed vessels: Middle cerebral artery, anterior cerebral artery, posterior cerebral artery, basilar artery, and vertebral arteries.
  • Clinical uses: Intraoperative cerebral blood flow monitoring, emboli detection, vasospasm assessment after subarachnoid hemorrhage, autoregulation assessment, and adjunctive testing in brain death confirmation.

Clinical Interpretation Snapshot

What TCD actually measures

TCD measures flow velocity, not absolute cerebral blood flow. Velocity changes are interpreted as a surrogate for cerebral perfusion only when vessel diameter is assumed to remain relatively constant.

Common waveform components

  • Peak systolic velocity: highest velocity during systole.
  • End-diastolic velocity: velocity at the end of diastole.
  • Mean flow velocity: average velocity across the cardiac cycle.
  • Pulsatility index: reflects downstream resistance and intracranial compliance trends.
