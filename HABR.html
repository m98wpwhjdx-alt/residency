Hepatic Arterial Buffer Response

A concise physiology explainer for anaesthesia, ICU, surgery, and exam revision.

Core concept

What is the hepatic buffer?

The hepatic buffer usually refers to the hepatic arterial buffer response.

The liver has a dual blood supply: the portal vein and the hepatic artery. When portal venous flow falls, hepatic arterial flow increases to partially maintain total liver blood flow.

↓ Portal venous flow → ↑ Hepatic arterial flow

Dual blood supply of the liver

Source Approximate flow contribution Main role
Portal vein 70–75% Nutrient-rich splanchnic venous blood
Hepatic artery 25–30% Oxygen-rich arterial blood

Mechanism: adenosine washout hypothesis

The classic mechanism is based on adenosine, which is produced around the portal triads near hepatic arterial resistance vessels. Normal portal flow washes adenosine away. When portal flow decreases, less adenosine is washed out, so local adenosine concentration rises and causes hepatic arterial vasodilation.

Why it matters

The liver cannot directly control portal venous inflow because portal flow depends on splanchnic venous drainage. The hepatic artery therefore compensates locally to help preserve liver perfusion.

This response is semi-reciprocal: if portal flow falls, hepatic arterial flow rises; if portal flow rises, hepatic arterial flow may fall.

Clinical relevance

Hypovolaemia / shock

Reduced portal flow may trigger hepatic arterial compensation, but the reserve is limited during severe low-flow states.

Liver transplantation

Excessive portal inflow can reduce hepatic arterial flow through the buffer response, contributing to hepatic artery hypoperfusion.

Cirrhosis / portal hypertension

The response may be altered, and microcirculatory perfusion can remain poor despite apparent changes in large-vessel flow.

One-line exam answer

The hepatic arterial buffer response is the compensatory increase in hepatic arterial blood flow when portal venous flow decreases, mainly mediated by reduced washout and accumulation of adenosine around hepatic arterial resistance vessels.
