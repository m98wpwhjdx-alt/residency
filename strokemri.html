MRI Protocol for Thrombolysis Window & Mechanical Thrombectomy Triage

A practical bedside-to-scanner guide for deciding whether a patient should be escalated for IV thrombolysis, neurointervention mechanical thrombectomy, or further perfusion-based selection.

Core question

Do not ask only “How many hours?” Ask: how much brain is dead, how much is salvageable, and is there a removable clot?

Minimum imaging logic:
DWI + ADC = infarct core
DWI vs FLAIR = biological age
CTA/MRA = target clot
Perfusion = salvageable penumbra
4.5 hStandard IV thrombolysis window from last known well in eligible patients.
DWI+Very early infarct signal; compare with ADC to prove restriction.
FLAIR−With DWI+ supports hyperacute biological window in selected unknown-onset stroke.
6–24 hExtended thrombectomy selection when LVO + favorable tissue imaging.

Acute Stroke MRI Triage: Four Questions

01

Is there bleed?

NCCT or GRE/SWI excludes hemorrhage before thrombolysis.

02

Is there infarct core?

DWI bright + ADC dark = true restricted diffusion, i.e., acute infarct core.

03

Is it early?

DWI-positive and FLAIR-negative suggests DWI–FLAIR mismatch and supports recent onset.

04

Is there LVO?

CTA/MRA identifies ICA terminus, M1, proximal M2, basilar, or other target occlusion.

05

Is brain salvageable?

Perfusion imaging estimates mismatch: small core with large penumbra favors reperfusion.

When MRI is especially useful

MRI is especially helpful in wake-up stroke, unknown-onset stroke, posterior circulation stroke, stroke mimics, and biological-window assessment using DWI–FLAIR mismatch.

Operational rule: If CT/CTA/CTP is faster and available, do not delay reperfusion just to obtain MRI in an otherwise clear stroke pathway.

How to Assess the Thrombolysis Window

1. Establish last known well

Use the last time the patient was definitely neurologically normal, not the time symptoms were discovered.

2. Read DWI + ADC first

DWI bright with ADC dark confirms acute infarct core. Large core increases hemorrhagic transformation risk.

3. Compare DWI with FLAIR

DWI-positive / FLAIR-negative = mismatch. In selected wake-up or unknown-onset stroke, this supports thrombolysis discussion.

4. Screen contraindications

Check BP, glucose, anticoagulation, recent surgery/bleeding, platelets/coagulation when clinically relevant, and local stroke protocol.

Wake-up / unknown-onset logic

If clock time excludes the patient but MRI shows DWI lesion smaller than one-third MCA territory and no visible corresponding FLAIR change, urgent stroke-team discussion for MRI-guided thrombolysis is appropriate in selected eligible patients.

Anaesthesia / Critical Care Checklist Before MRI or IR Suite

Stroke pathway data

Last known well, discovery time, NIHSS/deficit, GCS, glucose, BP, anticoagulant/antiplatelet history, recent surgery/bleeding, platelets/INR/aPTT when relevant.

Physiology

Maintain oxygenation, avoid hypotension, treat glucose extremes, assess aspiration risk and need for intubation before transport/scanner.

BP memory

For IV thrombolysis: usually <185/110 mmHg before treatment and <180/105 mmHg after. For thrombectomy: avoid hypotension; preserve collateral perfusion.

IR posting rule: IV thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy are not mutually exclusive. If eligible for IV lytic and LVO is present, the lytic should not delay transfer for thrombectomy.
