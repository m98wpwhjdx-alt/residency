Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

Clinical Overview & Critical Pathways

Definition

Massive transfusion is typically defined as the transfusion of ≥ 10 units of red blood cells (RBCs) within 24 hours or ≥ 40 mL/kg within 24 hours.

MTP is activated in cases of severe hemorrhage to rapidly replace blood volume and correct coagulopathy.

3. Adjunctive Therapies

  • Fibrinogen Replacement: Maintain levels at 1.5-2 g/L using fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate.
  • Tranexamic Acid (TXA): Early administration reduces bleeding by inhibiting fibrinolysis.
  • Calcium Supplementation: Citrated blood products cause hypocalcemia; maintain normocalcemia actively.
  • PCCs: Used for rapid correction of coagulopathy, especially vitamin K antagonist reversal.

4. Monitoring & Guidance

  • Transition to laboratory-guided therapy as soon as routine coagulation assays or viscoelastic tests are available.
  • Monitor hemoglobin, platelet count, coagulation parameters, fibrinogen, and calcium levels continuously.
Viscoelastic Testing: Utilize tools like ROTEM to guide targeted transfusion and specific factor replacement.

Risks & Complications

  • Transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI)
  • Circulatory overload (TACO)
  • Thromboembolic complications
  • Hypocalcemia resulting from citrate toxicity

Pediatric Considerations

  • Pediatric MTPs are less standardized; however, ratios similar to adults (1:1:1) are frequently deployed.
  • Low-titer group O whole blood (LTOB) is rapidly gaining favor for faster resuscitation and reduced donor exposures.
